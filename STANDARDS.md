## Source-first standard

`terra-legal` must not fabricate legal, licensing, governance, identity, or continuity structures in isolation.

Before adding or changing a rule, license, policy, directive, name, identity element, or bridge:

1. read the current repository surface;
2. identify external and internal donor sources;
3. identify ownership, contributors, third-party rights, and existing grants where relevant;
4. distinguish evidence, interpretation, strategy, policy, and legal advice;
5. record unresolved assumptions.

## Licensing standard

`terra-legal` is a multi-license repository.

- no single root license silently governs every file;
- file-level SPDX, adjacent `.license` files, and `REUSE.toml` define scope;
- canonical standard license texts belong in `LICENSES/`;
- unannotated historical files remain pending classification;
- third-party materials retain their original rights;
- earlier grants are not represented as revoked;
- Universal Convention and Responsible Use Policy do not become hidden additional restrictions on standard public licenses;
- logos, names, certification marks, privacy duties, and commercial terms remain separate legal layers.

## Local obligations

- keep the public surface readable for human review first;
- keep provenance and applicable rights visible;
- preserve separation among donor text, bridge text, policy, legal scope notice, archived draft, and repository-local instruction;
- do not flatten theory, archive, governance, licensing, audit, identity, and tooling into one undifferentiated file;
- do not introduce fake DOI, fake authority, unverifiable claims, or unreviewed public names;
- preserve historical version and license continuity;
- keep child safety, Detox, explicit operator control, privacy, and anti-surveillance safeguards intact;
- record material changes in TraceLog;
- apply naming, rights, third-party, accessibility, and visual-identity checks before public release;
- require professional review for jurisdiction-specific or materially binding custom legal terms.

## Packaging rule

Normative packaging follows this structure:

- exact canonical license texts where exact wording is required;
- file-level rights and provenance records;
- bridge files where cross-repository coherence matters;
- explicit notes where extraction, condensation, translation, or adaptation occurred;
- public TraceLog records for material documentary changes;
- identity files that improve legibility without importing promotional residue;
- exclusions for third-party, confidential, private, and rights-unresolved material.

## Completion rule

A file or release is not legally stabilized merely because it has a `LICENSE` file.

Completion requires coherent scope, ownership, contributor rights, third-party treatment, metadata, provenance, public notices, validation, and release behavior.
