# Terra Publication Rule

This file defines the publication boundary and publication behavior for the Terra legal donor repository.

## What belongs here

- stable legal texts;
- governance documents;
- participation rules;
- security and reporting guidance;
- bridge documents that other repositories can inherit;
- extracted documentary protocol surfaces with stated donor relation;
- naming, identity, TraceLog, and continuity bridge files that govern public Terra surfaces.

## What does not belong here

- raw archive dumps;
- duplicate theory corpora;
- temporary exports;
- auto-generated noise;
- private operational material;
- false complete-package claims for materials that belong to theory, corpus, or archive layers;
- unreviewed names presented as approved;
- promotional visual material presented as legal authority;
- full Invisible Shadow theory copied into the legal donor layer.

## Publication principles

Public material should be:

1. intentional;
2. readable;
3. reviewable by humans;
4. safe for open publication;
5. linked to a clear repository role;
6. traceable to donor sources where extraction occurred;
7. packaged in a way that preserves documentary meaning rather than flattening it;
8. consistent with the naming and brand identity standards;
9. recorded in TraceLog when the change is material;
10. compatible with Invisible Shadow anti-capture safeguards.

## Naming publication rule

Before a new public name appears in a release, site, repository title, badge, or publication package:

- create or update a Name Card;
- state approval status;
- preserve aliases and deprecated forms;
- separate language review from legal review;
- link the decision to TraceLog.

## Identity publication rule

Public design should:

- follow `BRAND_IDENTITY_STANDARD.md`;
- use `VISUAL_IDENTITY_GUIDE.md` and the shared stylesheet where applicable;
- remain readable without special fonts or JavaScript;
- avoid excessive motion and promotional residue;
- never imply registration, certification, or scientific proof through design alone.

## Continuity publication rule

A release should preserve:

- source and derivative relation;
- donor map;
- aliases and migration notes;
- unresolved questions and limitations;
- protected handling of private or sensitive material.

## Living publication rule

Publication surfaces in Terra are living.
They may stabilize temporarily for release or archival deposit, but they must not pretend that one snapshot exhausts the whole system.

## Cross-repository rule

If a file is primarily theoretical, move or mirror it into a theory repository.
If it is primarily a corpus or evidence object, keep it in a corpus repository.
If it is primarily a documentary donor rule, bridge, or normative surface, `terra-legal` may hold it.
If it is only shadow or backup material, keep it outside the public legal layer.

## Packaging rule

Publication inside `terra-legal` should follow:

- `FULL_PACKAGING_PROTOCOL.md`;
- `RULE_0_SOURCE_FIRST_PROTOCOL.md`;
- `DETOX_ENGINE_PROTOCOL.md`;
- `TRACELOG_PROTOCOL.md`;
- `NAMING_STANDARD.md`;
- `BRAND_IDENTITY_STANDARD.md`;
- `INVISIBLE_SHADOW_CONTINUITY_BRIDGE.md`.

No public package should be assembled here without role clarity, provenance, donor mapping, naming status, and a material-change trace.

## Protected decision boundary

License choice, authorship, copyright identity, and jurisdictional claims require a separate operator decision and legal review where appropriate. Publication packaging must not conceal unresolved conflict among these surfaces.
