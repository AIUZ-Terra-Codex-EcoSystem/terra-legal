## Stabilization note

This document preserves the Terra Public License concept as a draft responsible-technology instrument. It is not the active blanket license of `terra-legal` or of the wider Terra portfolio.

The complete pre-stabilization text is preserved in Git history at:

`https://github.com/AIUZ-Terra-Codex-EcoSystem/terra-legal/blob/46460fd1490c927bd007cbc08d138ca2f5a6e2d5/LICENSE.md`

The draft must not be copied into another repository as a production license unless a later version has undergone rights clearance, professional legal review, explicit approval, and repository-specific adoption.

## Intended purpose

The draft was designed for ethically oriented educational and high-responsibility technologies where child safety, data protection, and human dignity are treated as core duties.

Its intended goals included:

- open and collaborative use where legitimate;
- explicit child-protection duties;
- restrictions against exploitative or manipulative use;
- privacy and human-oversight expectations;
- responsible commercial review for high-impact deployment.

## Sources originally referenced

The draft referred to instruments and policy sources including:

- United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child;
- GDPR Article 8;
- COPPA;
- UNESCO Recommendation on the Ethics of Artificial Intelligence;
- Terra Universal Convention.

Reference to these sources does not by itself make the draft enforceable in every jurisdiction or suitable for every object class.

## Core principle retained

**Child Safety First**

Terra-managed child-facing work should avoid harm to children's physical, psychological, informational, economic, and intellectual development and should use proportionate privacy, security, transparency, and human-oversight safeguards.

## Principles retained for separate policy and contract work

- protection against sale or exploitation of child-related data;
- rejection of manipulative or compulsive mechanics directed at children;
- meaningful parental or guardian controls where required and appropriate;
- transparency, incident reporting, correction, and deletion pathways;
- security and safety review;
- human accountability and appeal;
- cultural and linguistic respect;
- responsible commercial and partner review.

These principles are now developed primarily through:

- `RESPONSIBLE_USE_POLICY.md`;
- `CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md`;
- `SECURITY.md`;
- `TERRA_UNIVERSAL_CONVENTION.md`;
- future privacy, child-safety, partner, service, and commercial agreements.

## Why the draft was removed from the root-license role

The earlier draft combined several distinct functions:

- copyright permission;
- software licensing;
- ethical-use restrictions;
- child-safety policy;
- privacy obligations;
- commercial pricing examples;
- contributor terms;
- enforcement language;
- cultural and environmental principles;
- Universal Convention alignment.

These functions require separate instruments, scope, evidence, and enforcement mechanisms.

The stabilized repository therefore separates:

- `LICENSE` and `LEGAL.md` — repository rights and licensing scope;
- `LICENSES/` — canonical standard license texts;
- `REUSE.toml` and file-level notices — machine-readable scope;
- `RESPONSIBLE_USE_POLICY.md` — ethical and safety expectations;
- `TRADEMARK_AND_BRAND_POLICY.md` — name, mark, endorsement, and certification boundaries;
- `CONTRIBUTOR_TERMS.md` — contributor certification and license grant;
- `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` — external rights and exclusions;
- future commercial and partner agreements — binding deployment terms where appropriate.

## Legacy commercial examples

Earlier versions included example revenue thresholds, annual fees, and revenue-sharing percentages.

Those examples are not active licensing terms, public offers, or approved commercial conditions. Any future commercial terms require a separate reviewed agreement identifying parties, scope, fees, taxes, governing law, warranties, liability, termination, and dispute resolution.

## Open-source status

The draft included field-of-use and ethical restrictions. It must not be described as an OSI-approved open-source license unless a future text independently satisfies the relevant definition and approval process.

## Future review requirements

Before any custom Terra license is used in production, review at minimum:

- the exact licensed object;
- the verified licensor and rights chain;
- contributor, co-author, employer, contractor, publisher, and assignment rights;
- compatibility with standard open-source and open-content licenses;
- patent and database-rights implications;
- governing law and dispute resolution;
- privacy and child-protection enforcement mechanisms;
- commercial terms and payment structure;
- trademark separation;
- warranty, liability, termination, notices, severability, and versioning;
- migration treatment for earlier versions and grants.

## Boundary

This file is a preserved legal-development draft and source artifact. It is not the repository's default license, not an approved open-source license, not a public commercial offer, and not a substitute for jurisdiction-specific legal advice.
