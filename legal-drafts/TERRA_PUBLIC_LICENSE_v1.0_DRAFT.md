## Stabilization note

This document preserves the Terra Public License concept as a draft responsible-technology instrument. It is no longer used as the default `terra-legal` repository license.

Its ethical content remains important as source material for:

- Terra Responsible Use Policy;
- child-safety requirements;
- partner and commercial agreements;
- Terra Universal Convention alignment;
- future professional legal review.

It must not be copied into other repositories as a production license unless a later reviewed and approved version is issued.

## Draft purpose

The draft was designed for ethically oriented educational technologies where child safety, data protection, and civilizational responsibility are treated as core duties.

Its intended goals were to combine:

- open and collaborative use where legitimate;
- explicit child-protection duties;
- ethical restrictions against exploitative use;
- a path for separate commercial licensing when public-good conditions are not sufficient.

## Legal foundation originally referenced

The draft referred to:

- UN Convention on the Rights of the Child;
- GDPR Article 8;
- COPPA;
- UNESCO AI Ethics Recommendation;
- Terra Universal Convention.

These references remain important policy sources, but their inclusion here does not automatically make the draft enforceable in every jurisdiction or for every repository object.

## Core principle

**Child Safety First**

Any legitimate Terra-linked educational or child-related deployment should improve children's lives or, at minimum, avoid harm to their physical, psychological, or intellectual development.

## Principal concepts retained for future policy drafting

- protection against commercial sale of children's data;
- rejection of manipulative or addictive mechanics aimed at children;
- prohibition of targeted advertising to children in Terra-controlled child-facing systems;
- meaningful parental control where child data is processed;
- transparency and incident reporting;
- security and safety review;
- human oversight;
- cultural and linguistic respect;
- responsible commercial review for high-impact deployment.

## Why this draft was separated from the root license

The original draft mixed several legal and governance functions:

- copyright permission;
- software licensing;
- child-safety policy;
- privacy obligations;
- commercial pricing examples;
- contributor terms;
- enforcement language;
- ethical and cultural principles;
- Terra Universal Convention alignment.

For stabilization, these functions are separated into clearer instruments:

- `LICENSE` and `LICENSE.md` for repository licensing scope;
- `LEGAL.md` for object-class licensing boundaries;
- `RESPONSIBLE_USE_POLICY.md` for ethical and safety obligations;
- `TRADEMARK_POLICY.md` for names, logos, certification, and identity;
- future commercial and partner agreements for binding deployment terms.

## Legacy pricing examples

Earlier draft versions contained example commercial thresholds and fee structures. These examples are not active public licensing terms for `terra-legal`.

Commercial terms, if ever used, must be placed in a separate reviewed agreement or commercial policy.

## Future review requirements

Before any future Terra custom license is used in production, it must be reviewed for:

- exact licensed object;
- legal identity of licensor;
- contributor and assignment rights;
- compatibility with standard open-source and open-content licenses;
- governing law and dispute resolution;
- privacy and child-protection enforcement mechanism;
- commercial terms and payment structure;
- trademark separation;
- warranty, liability, termination, notices, severability, and versioning.

## Boundary

This file is a preserved legal-development draft. It is not the repository's default license and should not be described as an approved open-source license.
