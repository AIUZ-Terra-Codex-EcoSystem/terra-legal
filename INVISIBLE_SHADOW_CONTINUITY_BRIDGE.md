## Purpose

The Invisible Shadow ontology describes continuity that remains active when explicit records, institutions, interfaces, or formal explanations are incomplete.

Inside `terra-legal`, this ontology is used only to define continuity safeguards. It is not copied here as a complete theory corpus.

## Repository-local meaning

The Invisible Shadow is the continuity layer that helps preserve:

- relations between source and derivative text;
- context lost during migration or compression;
- unresolved alternatives that should not be forced into false closure;
- recovery paths after fragmentation;
- human meaning that is not fully represented by file structure alone.

## Five normative functions

### 1. Silent archiving

Meaningful context may be preserved without turning every event into public data.

Silent archiving means disciplined preservation of necessary continuity, not hidden surveillance.

### 2. Contextual bridging

A bridge document must explain how one repository, protocol, publication, or archive depends on another.

Links alone are insufficient when the reason for the relation is not stated.

### 3. Long-term persistence

A documentary surface should remain interpretable after platform, filename, format, or organizational change.

Persistence requires provenance, aliases, reading paths, and repair history.

### 4. Anti-closure

An unresolved interpretation must not be presented as final merely because a document was published or a repository was released.

Living documents may stabilize for review while retaining visible uncertainty and open questions.

### 5. Anti-capture

Continuity must not become a pretext for compulsory monitoring, ownership of persons, behavioral profiling, or extraction of private life.

The trace belongs to meaning preservation, not control over the subject.

## Relationship to TraceLog

TraceLog preserves explicit documentary decisions.

Invisible Shadow preserves awareness that:

- not all relevant continuity has been recorded;
- absence of a record is not proof of absence;
- missing context should be marked rather than invented;
- restoration may require returning to original sources, people, material evidence, or archived states.

## Relationship to living and non-living systems

The broader Universal TraceLog concept may refer to intrinsic biological, molecular, material, cultural, or environmental signatures.

This repository does not operationalize or certify those scientific claims. Its local role is narrower: it prohibits reducing continuity to external labels, tracking devices, or administrative identifiers when the underlying source and material history are available.

## Required safeguards

Any continuity or trace surface must apply:

- data minimization;
- explicit role and purpose;
- provenance visibility;
- correction and protected-deletion paths;
- no-secret publication rules;
- child and vulnerable-person protection;
- distinction between evidence, interpretation, and hypothesis;
- Detox before, during, and after change.

## Use in audit

An audit should ask:

1. What continuity is visible?
2. What continuity is missing?
3. Was missing context honestly marked?
4. Did a migration preserve source and authorship?
5. Did simplification destroy meaning?
6. Did trace collection exceed the documentary purpose?
7. Is an archive or shadow layer pretending to be the current public surface?

## Boundary

This bridge does not:

- replace the full Invisible Shadow ontology;
- establish a scientific validation result;
- authorize hidden data collection;
- claim that all continuity can be formalized;
- treat the repository as the whole Terra system.
