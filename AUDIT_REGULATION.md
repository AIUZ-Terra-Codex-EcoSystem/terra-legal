# Terra Audit Regulation

This file defines the audit layer for documentary, repository, publication, naming, identity, TraceLog, and bridge surfaces within the Terra ecosystem.

## Purpose

Audit exists to verify that a public surface is:

- structurally coherent;
- documentary traceable;
- safe for publication;
- aligned with living directives and repository role;
- free from hidden fragmentation, silent drift, or public-facing distortion.

## Audit scope

Use this regulation when reviewing:

- repository root surfaces;
- living indexes;
- directives and protocol files;
- publication-facing bridge documents;
- naming records and registries;
- brand and visual-identity surfaces;
- TraceLog records;
- Invisible Shadow continuity bridges;
- release bundles and documentary packages;
- audit, validation, and governance files themselves.

## Non-negotiable audit rules

### 1. Source-first rule

No audit may certify a surface assembled without reading the relevant donor corpus.

### 2. No implicit action rule

Audit may identify problems and prepare repair surfaces, but it must not silently convert analysis into unannounced structural change.

### 3. Human-readable rule

Audit outputs must remain reviewable by humans and must not collapse into opaque machine-only diagnostics.

### 4. Child-safety and dignity rule

Any surface that weakens child safety, dignity, privacy, or anti-manipulation safeguards fails audit regardless of formatting quality.

### 5. Naming integrity rule

A public name fails audit when its origin, role, language review, aliases, decision, or limitations are hidden.

### 6. Identity integrity rule

A visual surface fails audit when design obscures legal meaning, simulates unsupported authority, reduces accessibility, or imports unreviewed promotional claims.

### 7. Trace and anti-capture rule

A trace surface fails audit when it records secrets, unnecessary personal data, hidden behavioral inference, or monitoring beyond its declared documentary purpose.

## Audit levels

- `surface`
  - readability, links, entry path, role clarity, naming and visual consistency
- `structural`
  - layer boundaries, donor relations, provenance, duplication, continuity
- `publication`
  - citation, evidence relation, packaging role, public safety, trace visibility
- `protocol`
  - consistency with directives, Rule 0, Detox, TraceLog, naming, identity, and living protocol rules

## Minimal audit sequence

1. Identify the target surface and declared repository role.
2. Read the relevant donor rules, directives, or protocol sources.
3. Check whether the surface matches its declared role.
4. Check for mojibake, dead links, false exhaustiveness, broken provenance, or archive leakage.
5. Check naming origin, aliases, and approval status.
6. Check accessibility and visual-identity compliance.
7. Check whether continuity is preserved without excessive capture.
8. Check whether the surface contradicts:
   - Rule 0;
   - Detox Engine logic;
   - explicit-command operation;
   - TraceLog protocol;
   - Invisible Shadow anti-capture safeguards;
   - living protocol rules.
9. State the audit result and required repair path.

## Required audit output

An audit output should identify:

- target surface;
- repository role;
- audit level used;
- detected fragmentation or risk;
- TraceLog reference;
- required action:
  - keep;
  - clarify;
  - detox;
  - extract;
  - re-layer;
  - relocate;
  - deprecate;
  - block pending operator decision.

## Immediate audit failure

- fabricated provenance;
- public secrets;
- false claims that a partial surface is the whole system;
- broken donor references presented as valid;
- machine-generated noise presented as documentary authority;
- assistant initiative replacing operator intent;
- unreviewed names presented as approved;
- identity elements presented as legal registration or scientific proof;
- TraceLog used for hidden monitoring;
- unresolved license conflict hidden behind a misleading license badge.
